# Scientific limitations

**SIMULATED DATA — NOT EXPERIMENTALLY VALIDATED · נתונים מדומים — לא אומתו בניסוי**

Olfactory Space Explorer (OSE) is a research *prototype*. Everything it ships with is generated by a fixed mathematical teacher model (`backend/app/datasets/synthetic.py`, seed 42). The interface labels provenance permanently, but the points below should be read before drawing any conclusion from what the app shows.

## 1. Synthetic receptor channels are not identified human receptors

The 64 channels `R001…R064` are computational channels whose tuning is drawn from random latent vectors. They do not correspond to any OR gene, have no measured ligand spectra, and their number (64) was chosen for CPU speed. Humans express roughly 400 functional OR types; the architecture can be configured for that (`ModelConfig.n_receptors`), but no receptor data are included. The teacher's assay noise (σ = 0.005) is simulated.

## 2. Learned odor embeddings are not measurements of subjective experience

`z ∈ ℝ²⁵⁶` is the output of a network trained to reproduce synthetic receptor responses, synthetic descriptor probabilities, synthetic intensity and a synthetic similarity structure. The ten descriptor names (fruity, floral, …) are labels for teacher outputs, not human ratings. "Similarity" means cosine similarity of a hidden teacher vector, not human similarity judgments. Nothing in the embedding has been compared with what anyone smells.

## 3. Projections distort high-dimensional geometry

PCA keeps the three directions of largest variance in the fixed reference atlas; in the shipped model they explain about 20.5 %, 12.9 % and 10.3 % of the variance (≈ 44 % together). More than half of the variance — and with it many distances — is invisible in the 3-D view. Two points that look close may be far apart in ℝ²⁵⁶ and vice versa. Direct-dimension and weighted views show only 3 of 256 coordinates (or 3 linear combinations). The "neighbours (256D)" lines in the viewer connect the true nearest neighbours in the full space to make this distortion visible. The PCA basis is fitted once and never refitted while inputs change, so movement in the view reflects changes in the model output, not changes in the viewpoint.

## 4. The binding model is a simplified approximation

The receptor layer uses a competitive-binding steady state, `r_j = Σᵢ εᵢⱼ cᵢ/Kᵢⱼ / (1 + Σᵢ cᵢ/Kᵢⱼ)`. It ignores receptor dynamics, desensitization, allosteric effects, odorant-binding proteins, mucus partitioning, air-phase transport and non-competitive antagonism. The synthetic teacher deliberately contains effects the student cannot represent exactly (receptor-specific Hill exponents and a pairwise synergy/suppression term), so the reported test error is a genuine approximation gap — but real chemistry will contain many more.

Concentrations are in arbitrary units; the training range is 0.02–2.0. Values above 2 are extrapolations and the interface warns about them.

## 5. The cortical network is biologically inspired, not a brain simulation

The "olfactory bulb" is a normalized excitatory/inhibitory linear-nonlinear layer with leaky integration (τ ∈ [0.02, 0.4] s). The "piriform cortex" is a GRU with top-k sparsification (20 % kept) followed by a residual MLP. These are inductive biases, not models of mitral/tufted cells, granule-cell circuits, centrifugal feedback or piriform association fibres. Time constants are learned only indirectly (training uses steady-state stimuli), so temporal trajectories are *model behaviour under the chosen protocol*, not predictions of neural recordings.

Inference-time switches (lateral inhibition off, different sparsity, receptor knockdown) are counterfactual manipulations that never occurred during training; the interface flags them as such.

## 6. Inverse optimization is not physical odor reproduction

Inverse Design minimizes `‖F(c) − z_target‖² + λΩ(c)` over bounded concentrations using the trained forward model. A small predicted distance means only that *this model* maps the candidate close to the target. The problem is not unique: different starts routinely reach different mixtures with similar distances, and the response reports how many distinct component sets lie within 25 % of the best. A target obtained by dragging in 3-D is lifted to ℝ²⁵⁶ with the minimum-norm rule `Δz = P⁺Δp`, which is one choice among infinitely many latent vectors sharing the same projection, and it may lie off the manifold of reachable embeddings (an out-of-distribution warning is shown).

## 7. Hilbert-space notation does not imply quantum computation

`ℋ_M ⊗ ℋ_E → ℋ_R → ℋ_B → ℋ_P` names classical, finite-dimensional real feature spaces. The tensor-product notation describes how molecular identity and concentration combine; there is no quantum state, superposition or quantum algorithm anywhere in the code.

## 8. Real-data validation is required before any predictive claim

All reported metrics (e.g. receptor R² 0.937 on unseen mixtures, 0.860 on held-out compounds) quantify how well the student approximates a teacher that *we wrote*. They say nothing about olfactory biology. Predictive claims would require, at minimum: real receptor assays or human psychophysics imported through the schema-validated importer (`backend/app/datasets/importer.py`), proper train/test protocols on that data, calibration of uncertainty, and replication. No calibration results exist and none are claimed.

## Other caveats

* **Fourier analysis** describes temporal structure of a simulated or measured signal (e.g. a 4 Hz sniffing protocol). Frequencies say nothing about molecular identity. With 120 frames over 4 s the resolution is 0.25 Hz and the Nyquist frequency 15 Hz.
* **Taylor expansions** are local. The panel shows the error growing away from c₀; it also reports the agreement between autograd and finite-difference derivatives as a numerical check.
* **Resting state.** The model was trained only on odor-present stimuli. The "rest" (no-odor) embedding at t = 0 of a trajectory lies far from all reference odors; that distance is a property of the model, not a measured baseline.
* **Missing labels** in the synthetic data (10 % of descriptor rows, 15 % of intensity values) are deliberately introduced to exercise masking; they are never replaced by zero.
